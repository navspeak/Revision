BINOMIAL DISTRIBUTION
======================

Conditions (ALL four must hold)
---------------------------------
1. Fixed number of trials (n)
2. Each trial has exactly two outcomes — success or failure
3. Probability of success (p) is constant across all trials
4. Trials are independent

  Applicable                              Not Applicable
  ─────────────────────────────────────   ──────────────────────────────────────
  Toss coin 20 times → count tails        Toss coin UNTIL heads (n not fixed)
  Ask 200 people: older than 21? (Y/N)    Ask 200 people: how old are you? (not binary)
  Draw 4 balls WITH replacement           Draw 4 balls WITHOUT replacement (p changes)


Formula
--------
  P(X = r) = nCr × p^r × (1-p)^(n-r)

  Where:
    n   = total number of trials
    r   = number of successes you want
    p   = probability of success in one trial
    nCr = n! / (r! × (n-r)!)  — number of ways to arrange r successes in n trials


Worked Example — Car Licence Plate
------------------------------------
Last digit of a car licence plate is equally likely to be even or odd.
You observe 10 cars. What is the probability that exactly 5 have an even last digit?

  n = 10  (10 cars observed)
  r = 5   (want exactly 5 even)
  p = 0.5 (even or odd equally likely)

  P(X = 5) = C(10,5) × (0.5)^5 × (0.5)^5

  C(10,5) = 10! / (5! × 5!) = 252

  P(X = 5) = 252 × (0.5)^10
           = 252 / 1024
           ≈ 0.246  (24.6%)


Expected Value and Variance for Binomial
------------------------------------------
  E(X)   = n × p
  Var(X) = n × p × (1-p)
  SD(X)  = √(n × p × (1-p))

  For the licence plate example:
  E(X)   = 10 × 0.5 = 5        (expect 5 even plates on average)
  Var(X) = 10 × 0.5 × 0.5 = 2.5
  SD(X)  = √2.5 ≈ 1.58


SAMPLE QUESTIONS
=================

Q1. A fair coin is tossed 8 times. What is the probability of getting exactly 3 heads?
  n=8, r=3, p=0.5
  P(X=3) = C(8,3) × (0.5)^3 × (0.5)^5
          = 56 × (0.5)^8
          = 56/256
          ≈ 0.219  (21.9%)

Q2. A call centre agent resolves 70% of calls successfully. Out of 5 calls, what is
    the probability that exactly 4 are resolved?
  n=5, r=4, p=0.7
  P(X=4) = C(5,4) × (0.7)^4 × (0.3)^1
          = 5 × 0.2401 × 0.3
          = 5 × 0.07203
          ≈ 0.360  (36.0%)

Q3. A factory has a 10% defect rate. A batch of 6 items is inspected.
    What is the probability of finding at most 1 defective item?
  n=6, p=0.1
  P(X ≤ 1) = P(X=0) + P(X=1)

  P(X=0) = C(6,0) × (0.1)^0 × (0.9)^6 = 1 × 1 × 0.531 = 0.531
  P(X=1) = C(6,1) × (0.1)^1 × (0.9)^5 = 6 × 0.1 × 0.590 = 0.354

  P(X ≤ 1) = 0.531 + 0.354 = 0.885  (88.5%)
  (This introduces Cumulative Probability — covered next)

Q4. Is binomial applicable? Why / why not?
  a) Rolling a die 10 times, counting how many times you get a 6     → YES (binary: 6 or not 6, fixed n, constant p)
  b) Drawing cards without replacement until you get an Ace          → NO  (n not fixed, p changes each draw)
  c) Surveying 50 people: do you own a car? (yes/no)                 → YES (binary, fixed n, independent)
  d) Recording the exact score of 20 students in an exam             → NO  (not binary — many outcomes)


Two Ways to Compute E(X) for Binomial — Both Give the Same Answer
------------------------------------------------------------------
Long way (from definition):
  E(X) = Σ x · P(X=x)
       = 0·P(X=0) + 1·P(X=1) + 2·P(X=2) + ... + n·P(X=n)

Shortcut (closed-form):
  E(X) = n × p

The shortcut is the mathematical proof that the full sum always simplifies to n×p.

Example — pasta packets (n=10, p=0.05):
  Long way: 0·P(X=0) + 1·P(X=1) + ... + 10·P(X=10) = 0.5
  Shortcut: 10 × 0.05 = 0.5  ✓

Use the long way to understand what E(X) means.
Use the shortcut to compute it fast.
